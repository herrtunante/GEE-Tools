About this viewer

On 26 August 2026 an outburst flood — preliminarily attributed to an ice and rock avalanche from a glacier in the upper catchment — swept the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river corridor across the Nepal–China border. This viewer streams Planet satellite imagery published through the Planet Crisis Response Program directly from Source Cooperative as Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs — no server required.

Imagery

  • Before: 5 PlanetScope scenes, 27 May 2026 (end of dry season), ~3.8 m, one strip from Trishuli Bazaar north to the Rasuwagadhi border crossing.
  • Band combinations: true colour uses the RGB visual assets; false colour (NIR·R·G) and NDVI stream the 4-band analytic assets (analytic_sr pre-event, analytic post-event, pansharpened SkySat) with a per-scene percentile stretch. Because pre-event is surface reflectance and post-event is top-of-atmosphere radiance, compare spatial patterns across epochs, not absolute values.
  • Loupe (press L): a magnifier that follows the cursor and shows the other epoch at +2 zoom — hover post-event debris to see what stood there before.
  • After (PlanetScope): 9 scenes, morning of 26 Aug 2026, ~3.8 m, two swaths.
  • After (SkySat): 2 collects, 27 Aug 2026 02:00 UTC, ~0.8 m, over the Rasuwagadhi border crossing and Syabrubesi.

Caveats

  • Cloud cover is significant (monsoon): pre-event 5–52%, post-event PlanetScope 62–93%, SkySat ~50%. Much of the obscuration is thin haze.
  • Post-event PlanetScope swaths extend beyond the single pre-event strip, so some post-event area has no baseline; SkySat covers two focal points only.
  • 13 of the 14 PlanetScope scenes carry quality_category: test in Planet's Data API; both SkySat collects are standard.
  • Casualty figures in the source dataset are provisional as of 26 Aug 2026.
